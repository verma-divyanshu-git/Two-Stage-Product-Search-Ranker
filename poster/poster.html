Two-Stage Product Search Ranker

Hybrid retrieval, a LightGBM cascade and a fine-tuned cross-encoder on the Shopping Queries (ESCI) dataset

Problem

Given a shopping query, rank products from a 482k catalog so the ones that exactly match come first, within a 100 ms budget.

Every labelled query-product pair carries one of four labels:

labelgainshare
EExact: the product matches the query1.043.5%
SSubstitute: close, but some detail differs0.135.1%
CComplement: goes with what was asked for0.014.5%
IIrrelevant016.9%

Data

US English queries, split by query so none lands in two splits. The cross-encoders never train on pairs LightGBM learns from.

Metrics

NDCG@k = DCG@k / ideal DCG@k,   DCG@k = Σi≤k gaini / log2(i+1)

Full-list NDCG scores each query's whole labelled list, the KDD Cup leaderboard metric. NDCG@10 looks only at what a shopper sees and is much stricter.

Recall@k is the share of Exact products that retrieval finds in its top k of the whole catalog.

System

Fine-tuning the cross-encoder

An MS MARCO cross-encoder reads query and title together. One epoch on 8.3k ce_train queries, about 5 minutes on a laptop GPU, teaches it ESCI's notion of a match.

Takeaways

  • Hybrid retrieval finds 5 points more Exact products than BM25 alone, because lexical and dense search miss different products.
  • Lexical features stall at 0.725 NDCG@10. Telling Exact from Substitute needs a model that reads both texts together.
  • The cascade spends the cross-encoder only on the 50 candidates the cheap ranker likes best.
  • Fine-tuning moved NDCG@10 more than any feature, and its score takes 68% of the full ranker's gain importance.
